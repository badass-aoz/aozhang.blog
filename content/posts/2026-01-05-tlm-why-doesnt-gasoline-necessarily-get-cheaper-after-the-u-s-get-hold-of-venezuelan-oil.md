Three Line Clarity:

A crude supply surge *sounds* bearish for gasoline,

but the link is weak and inconsistent.

Gas prices are shaped just as much by refinery capacity, fuel regulations, and logistics as by oil itself.

Context:

Crude oil is globally consumed, but gasoline is locally constrained due to its production. Even if Venezuelan crude increases supply on paper, much of it is heavy sour oil that only certain U.S. refineries can process. At the same time, refinery capacity is limited, maintenance cycles are tight, environmental fuel standards vary by region, and margins expand when refiners gain pricing power. The result: oil prices can fall while gasoline prices barely move—or even rise—because the true choke point isn’t oil in the ground, but fuel coming out of refineries.
